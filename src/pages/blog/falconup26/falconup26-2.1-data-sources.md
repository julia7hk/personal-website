### finance background:

OHLCV: 5 numbers that describe a stock's trading day:

- Open — price when the market opened
- High — highest price during the day
- Low — lowest price during the day
- Close — price when the market closed
- Volume — how many shares were traded

FRED: Federal Reserve Economic Data

free public database run by the St. Louis Fed with thousands of economic data series

- Fed funds rate — the "risk-free rate" used to calculate Sharpe ratio (basically: "how much better is this investment than just parking your money in a risk-free asset?")
- VIX — the market's "fear gauge," measures expected volatility of the S&P 500
- Treasury yield curves — useful for stress scenarios later

# last thing to do: sector/industry data

Two approaches:

1. Simple — just store sector for individual stocks, and tag ETFs with a broad category
(e.g., "broad market", "leveraged tech", "semiconductor"). Good enough to flag "hey, 80% of
your portfolio is tech-adjacent." Fast to build.
2. Detailed — pull actual ETF holdings/sector weights. yfinance has some of this via
`Ticker.funds_data`, but it's spotty. More accurate concentration analysis, but more work and
potentially unreliable data.

option 1 first, upgrade to option 2 later.

What was added:

1. A hardcoded cheat sheet for ETFs — quick lookup table:
QQQ → Technology, Broad Market Tech ETF
SOXL → Technology, Leveraged Semiconductor ETF
SPY → Broad Market, S&P 500 ETF
GLD → Commodities, Gold ETF
...17 total
2. For individual stocks, ask yfinance for sector

! The logic is: check the cheat sheet first → if not there, ask yfinance → if yfinance
doesn't know either, return "Unknown"

1. **New endpoint: `GET /api/symbols/QQQ/sector` returns:**
`{"symbol": "QQQ", "sector": "Technology", "industry": "Broad Market Tech ETF", "is_etf": true}`
2. Cache for 7 days because a company's sector basically never changes.
3. `is_etf` flag tells frontend whether it's

    real sector data (from yfinance) vs. category tag (from the cheat sheet)
